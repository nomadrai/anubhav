# Pilot plan

## Scope and sample

A future formative pilot should invite **5–10 consenting adults** who can understand the information sheet and complete the language/accessibility options. This is a small convenience sample. It cannot establish effectiveness, safety, representativeness, financial behavior change, or generalisation to a wider population.

Phase 0 contains only a `pilot=1` local summary scaffold. It does not collect, transmit, store, or analyse pilot responses. No pilot results exist.

## Consent and safety

Before any future session, explain the educational purpose, synthetic data, lack of advice, no expected financial benefit, what is recorded, who can see it, retention/deletion, voluntary participation, withdrawal, discomfort from loss examples, and contact/escalation route. Ask permission separately for direct quotes or recordings; default to no recording. `TODO(human): obtain appropriate review and consent wording for the actual setting.`

## Manual pre/post reactions

A facilitator may manually record, outside the Phase 0 app, short pre/post reactions using the approved form:

- Before: “What do you expect leverage to change?”
- After: “What changed in your understanding of exposure, loss, forced exit, or recovery?”
- Before and after: “How confident are you explaining the terms?” using a clearly labeled, non-diagnostic 1–5 response.
- After: one question the participant would ask a qualified source.

Do not ask participants for income, holdings, account identifiers, or investment recommendations. Do not calculate a causal effect from this sample. Summarise response counts and notable comprehension issues only after consent and privacy review; keep raw notes out of the repository.

## Success signals and limits

Useful formative signals are completion friction, misunderstood terms, keyboard/screen-reader barriers, and whether participants can restate that synthetic paths are not predictions. These are usability observations, not efficacy evidence. A summary must report sample size, recruitment context, missing responses, facilitator influence, and the small-sample limitation.
